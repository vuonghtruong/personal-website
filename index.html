Research

From biological clocks
to models of cognition.

My work asks how internal dynamics organize behavior—and how experiments and models can help us distinguish the mechanisms.

01

Circadian networks & adaptation

I study the suprachiasmatic nucleus under nonstandard light–dark schedules, or T-cycles, combining bioluminescence measurements, time-series analysis and oscillator models. I’m interested in how environmental timing shapes network dynamics and leaves persistent effects on biological rhythms.

SCN modeling preprint ↗Model code ↗
02

Rhythms across development & scales

I investigate how biological timing emerges across tissues and stages of development. This includes collaborative work on early choroid plexus clocks and tools that combine bioluminescence with heartbeat imaging to study rhythmic activity in embryonic cardiac tissue.

Developmental clock preprint ↗
03

Learning, inference & human cognition

My background in computational cognitive science includes Bayesian learning models and their relationship to fMRI and EEG signals. I also collaborate on models of human cognition and explore how time of day relates to patterns of semantic exploration in language.
